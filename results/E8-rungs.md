# E8 — what a consistency rung costs

Generated by `cargo run --release -p experiments -- e8`. Medians over five seeds (1, 7, 42, 100, 2024); 10,000 accounts, 40,000 operations, budget 5%, Zipf *s* = 0.9; identical workload across rungs.

`divergences` compares every served value against an independent fold at the anchor it was served with. A rung that is cheap because it drops deltas is not a cheap rung, and only a value check tells the two apart.

`checkpoint_interval` is C, the per-key bounding mechanism H-S3's proviso names. Every published E8 figure before cycle 13 was measured at C = 0, which is `Ledger::new()`'s default and is the configuration §9.4.1 says the cost law excludes. `checkpointed_keys` is of 10,000 and is the column that explains the rest: an interval above a key's history depth records nothing for that key.

**Reading it.** The rung's saving is in *passes*, not in deltas: a bounded rung is dragged to the frontier less often and folds the same deltas when it is. The read columns are not indistinguishable across rungs — a lax rung misses more and reconstructs more, because its entries are genuinely less current. Everything but the read column is invariant in C: a checkpoint changes what a reconstruction costs, not whether a read misses.
