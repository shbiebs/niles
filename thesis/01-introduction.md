# 1. Introduction

## 1.1 Motivation

Every institution that keeps a system of record keeps two kinds of truth. The first is the *record*: what happened, in what order, attributed and never revised. The second is the *summary*: what the record implies right now — an account balance, a position, a regulatory aggregate, a fraud feature. The record is written once and read forever; the summaries are recomputed constantly and forgotten freely. These two kinds of truth have opposite engineering economics, and nearly every production estate serves them with different machines glued together by pipelines.

The gluing is where the trouble starts. A typical core-banking estate runs a transactional database for postings, a change-data-capture pipeline into a message bus, a fleet of stream processors and caches for balances and statements, and a warehouse for analytics. Each hop introduces a new consistency domain with its own failure modes. The failure mode is not hypothetical or obscure: it is named in regulatory guidance. The FDIC and the CFPB both describe *authorize-positive-settle-negative* (APSN) fee practices, which arise precisely because two derived views of the same ledger — the *available balance*, which nets authorized-but-unsettled holds, and the *ledger balance*, which counts only settled transactions — can disagree at the moment a decision is made [FDIC FIL-19-2023; CFPB Circular 2022-06]. Two read models over one ledger, differing in exactly which pending facts they include, produce a consumer-harm pattern serious enough for supervisory attention. That is this thesis's problem statement written by a regulator.

Meanwhile the read side has a structure that current systems ignore rather than exploit. Across a realistic institution, derived read models number in the thousands. A bank must serve, from the same postings: a customer's *available balance* (posted minus holds) at authorization latency; the balance *as of any historical date*, on both the booking and the value-date axes; positions rolled up by product, currency, desk, legal entity and geography for risk reporting; the month-to-date statement; and real-time features for fraud and anti-money-laundering scoring. BCBS 239 makes the open-endedness of this family an explicit supervisory expectation: Principle 4 requires risk data to be available "by business line, legal entity, asset type, industry, region and other groupings," and Principle 6 requires the bank to satisfy "a broad range of on-demand, ad hoc risk management reporting requests, including requests during stress/crisis situations" [BCBS d328]. A view family that must answer *ad hoc* requests cannot be fully enumerated in advance, and therefore cannot be fully materialized in advance. This is the **read-model explosion**: the set of derived views is open, and the cost of materializing all of it is unbounded, while the cost of materializing none of it is paid on every read.

Access to that family is skewed, and the skew is the exploitable structure — but the literature disagrees about how much, and the disagreement matters enough to be a design premise rather than an assumption. Twitter's analysis of 153 in-memory cache clusters (~700 billion requests) found most workloads well fit by Zipf with α roughly in the range 1–2.5, and read-heavy workloads more skewed (median α ≈ 1.4) than write-heavy ones (median α ≈ 0.9) [Yang et al., OSDI '20]. Meta's CacheLib paper deliberately pushes back: of its four production workloads, only one is Zipfian with α near 1, while SocialGraph shows α ≈ 0.55 and the CDN workload α ≈ 0.7, and the authors state plainly that "prior models have been too optimistic about the cacheability of production workloads" [Berg et al., OSDI '20]. Any thesis claiming that demand-driven materialization pays must therefore *measure* where it pays rather than assume it, and must survive the flat end of that range. That requirement is what turns an engineering optimization into a scientific question with a frontier.

### 1.1.1 The Attempt That Started This

This work began with an engineering attempt rather than a research question. The author set out to build a core-banking system in Rust on Noria, whose partially-stateful dataflow looked like exactly the right read-side mechanism for the balance-and-statement family described above. That attempt never reached a running system, and the reason it failed is not the reason one might expect. It is worth stating precisely, because the honest version motivates this thesis better than the flattering one.

**Noria was never successfully built, started, or connected to.** Not once; no SQL statement was ever executed against it. The `noria` crate requires a 2021-era nightly Rust; it uses `impl_trait_in_assoc_type` in a form that modern nightly rejects; its transitive `librocksdb-sys` dependency panics under current LLVM's bindgen; no container image is published; and a source build pinned to the contemporaneous nightly still fails, because the crate's *unpinned transitive dependencies* have moved on. The attempt pivoted to Materialize, which refused `CREATE TABLE` with a primary key, and ended without an end-to-end run on any engine. The full transcript is analysed line by line in `docs/research/gbs-noria-postmortem.md`, including the parts that are unflattering to this narrative: a substantial share of the friction was neither Noria nor semantics but toolchain accident, and the source build omitted `--locked`, which is the textbook cause of precisely the failure observed.

So this thesis does **not** rest on a claim that Noria cannot serve a bank; that claim was never tested and §11.5.5 declines to make it. Two things were nonetheless learned, and both shaped what follows.

The first is about *artefacts*. A research system distributed only as a source tree pinned to a five-year-old toolchain, with unlocked dependencies and a C++ dependency broken by later compilers, is undeployable however good its design. This is why every engineering commitment in Chapter 7 — pinned toolchain, locked dependencies, determinism gates, a build that a stranger can reproduce — is treated as a research obligation rather than an operational nicety.

The second is directly on this thesis's subject, and it was visible in the code as it was written. **Every guarantee the engine could not express reappeared, immediately, as unverified application code.** When the engine refused primary keys, uniqueness became `transaction_id + 1` with a comment explaining that the increment keeps the key unique. Atomicity became a comment reading *"in a real banking app, you would wrap this in a SQL transaction."* And a failed balance read became `Err(_) => 0, // Account not found or zero balance` — a database error rendered silently as a zero balance, by a developer working around an engine with no way to say *"I do not know."* That last line is the "miss ≠ zero" defect this thesis's absence lattice exists to make unrepresentable, written spontaneously, in a real attempt, by someone who understood the problem. No currency column, no overdraft check and no audit metadata ever appeared in any schema.

That is an anecdote — one developer, one attempt, no control — and it is labelled as one here. It is not evidence and Chapter 9 does not treat it as such; the evidentiary work is done by the differential defect corpus of §6.10.3, which writes thirteen defect classes twice and counts which are caught and when. But it is the observation that turned an engineering project into this research question: if the guarantees a ledger needs keep migrating into application code the moment the system cannot hold them, then the interesting question is not which engine to choose but **what a system would have to be able to say** for them to stay put.

The motivation of this thesis is the conviction that this is not a systems-integration problem but a missing theory. If the authoritative history is an immutable, epoch-ordered, hash-chained base, and every read model is a *function of a prefix of that base*, then eviction and reconstruction become algebraic operations whose safety can be proved once, generically, instead of re-argued per pipeline. The database, the cache, the stream processor and the audit log collapse into one system with one semantics — provided we can state that semantics precisely, bound its costs, price each level of consistency, and give programmers a language in which the invariants are checkable before the program runs.

## 1.2 Existing Solutions

Existing systems each solve a projection of the problem; none solves its whole. Chapter 2 gives the technical background and Chapter 10 the detailed differences; what follows is the shape of the gap.

**Transactional and distributed SQL databases** (PostgreSQL, MySQL; Spanner, CockroachDB, FoundationDB, and the deterministic school of Calvin and Aria) provide serializable or strictly serializable writes and a general query surface. Their derived state, however, is either recomputed per query or maintained as *totally* materialized views with eager maintenance; history is disposable (MVCC versions are garbage-collected, audit is bolted on via triggers or WAL archaeology); and SQL cannot express "this view may be two epochs stale" or "this transaction must balance per currency" as checkable properties. Calvin and Aria are important positive precedent rather than competition: both show that pre-agreeing a total order over transaction *inputs* turns distributed commit into deterministic local replay [Thomson et al., SIGMOD '12; Lu et al., PVLDB '20], which is exactly the property this thesis's epoch-ordered ledger supplies to the read side.

**Purpose-built ledgers** get the write path right and stop there. TigerBeetle's data model is accounts, transfers and ledgers, with the transfer `id` serving as an idempotency key, per-account flags such as `credits_must_not_exceed_debits` expressing non-negativity as a first-class constraint, and two-phase *pending transfers* that reserve funds in `debits_pending`/`credits_pending` and are resolved by *appending a new transfer* rather than mutating the original [TigerBeetle docs]. All data is "immutable, checksummed, and hash-chained." That is a ledger-grade write path with a fixed schema and point-lookup reads: there is no general query surface, no derived-view runtime, no language. Uber's LedgerStore is the strongest industrial evidence for this thesis's read side: over one immutable, verifiable ledger it ships *three different index classes* — strongly consistent indexes (two-phase commit, used for credit-card authorization holds), eventually consistent indexes (payment history), and time-range indexes for tiering [Uber Engineering]. Different consistency for different derived views over one immutable base is already production practice; what is missing is a semantics for it. Square's "Books" makes the same architectural choice — journal entries must balance to zero, tables have "no update statements … only inserts," and the payout balance is deliberately *materialized* rather than aggregated [Square Developer Blog]. Immutable and bitemporal databases (Datomic, XTDB) demonstrate that retention is affordable and gives time travel almost free, but their read sides are totally materialized indexes or per-query evaluation.

**Incremental view maintenance and streaming engines** (Materialize, Feldera/DBSP, Flink, RisingWave) maintain derived views incrementally with strong internal consistency. Materialize is the sharpest comparison because it already ships a consistency ladder: strict serializable (the default), serializable, and bounded staleness parameterized by a duration, with documentation that is explicit about what serializable permits — a transaction reading an expensive materialized view "may not observe all rows visible to" a transaction that read the underlying table moments earlier [Materialize docs]. The differentiating dimension is therefore not consistency levels, which exist, but **partiality**: these engines materialize views *totally*, so state grows with the domain of the view rather than with what is actually read, and the authoritative write path lives in someone else's system.

**Partially stateful dataflow** — Noria — pioneered exactly the right read-side mechanism: operators and views may contain holes; a read that hits a hole issues an *upquery* toward the base tables; eviction re-creates holes [Gjengset et al., OSDI '18]. Noria reports that partial state reduced memory to roughly 9% of full state in its Lobsters deployment and supported roughly 5× the load of a hand-optimized MySQL baseline in that application, at 16 vCPUs and a sub-100 ms 95th-percentile SLO. It is also explicit about its limits: "It only guarantees eventual consistency; its eviction from partial state is randomized; … and it lacks support for some SQL keywords." Gjengset's dissertation names the three invariants partial state must maintain and the five anomalies it must exclude — double application, skipped deltas, upquery races, lost deltas, upquery deadlock — and lists transactions and stronger consistency as *future work*. That list is the negative space this thesis occupies.

**Event sourcing, CQRS, and the log-centric school** assert the right shape — immutable log, derived projections — as a discipline imposed on developers rather than a system with a semantics. Notably, both Flink and Kafka achieve "exactly once" by committing derived state and input position *atomically* [Carbone et al.; Kafka KIP-98], which is structurally the same move as anchoring a read model to a ledger epoch; the industrial pattern exists, the general theory does not. Structurally identical, too, is Trillian's *log-backed map*: a verifiable map whose changes are only applied once present in a verifiable log [Trillian]. And Lucene has run the immutable-segments-plus-tombstones-plus-background-merge architecture in production for two decades: "Lucene indexes are 'write-once' files: once a segment has been written … it is never altered" [Apache Solr Reference Guide]. The shape is not in dispute. What is missing is partiality with a proof.

**Language-level attempts.** No language in SQL's lineage types money, time, staleness, idempotency, retention, or conservation. Among SQL alternatives — Datalog and DDlog, PRQL, Malloy, EdgeQL, Morel, Logica, KQL, Rel — a survey of their own stated positioning finds that *not one* claims formal SQL-superset expressive power; several (PRQL, Malloy, Logica) are bounded above by SQL because they compile to it, and KQL is read-only by design. Rel is the closest in ambition, arguing for a full language rather than a sublanguage, but it argues by design philosophy rather than by theorem [Aref et al., 2025]. The type-theoretic machinery needed to close the gap exists and is proven — units-of-measure types with a parametricity theorem [Kennedy, POPL '97], linear and affine types [Wadler; RustBelt], row-based effect systems [Leijen, Koka], refinement types [Rondon et al.], information-flow typing with non-interference [Denning; Sabelfeld & Myers] — but it has never been connected to a database runtime's consistency and eviction machinery.

**The synthesis gap.** Assembled, these families cover every ingredient and no combination: strictly serializable ordered writes without read-side economics; incremental reads without partiality or without authority; partiality without consistency or invariants; ledgers without generality or language; language rigor without a database semantics to be sound *for*; architecture without proof.

## 1.3 Problem Statement

**Problem.** Serve write-heavy and read-heavy workloads coherently from a single semantic foundation, such that:

1. the write path is an authoritative, append-only, strictly serializable, hash-chained, epoch-ordered base that is never partial and never forgets — in the general case a committed write to an authoritative base relation, and in the banking domain layer a balanced double-entry posting;
2. the read path is a large, open-ended, skewed family of derived read models maintained incrementally and *partially* — materializing only what is read, evicting the rest, and reconstructing evicted entries on demand via upqueries that flow up the dataflow toward the base — while each read is served at a *declared* consistency level anchored to the base's global visibility timeline;
3. the choice of materialization mode per view and per key range — absent, demand-materialized, fully materialized, spilled, tiered — is made by an algorithm against a cost model, subject to each view's consistency and freshness contract, rather than by a human guess encoded in a deployment;
4. every operation, change and state is traceable: the system can say what an answer was, as of which epoch, and from which base facts it was derived;
5. the whole is bound by strict-serializable correctness, durability, immutability, auditability and retention, with domain invariants (double-entry balance, currency safety, idempotency, authorization) preserved by construction and checkable before execution; and
6. none of this costs generality: the engine must serve the workload classes a general-purpose DBMS serves — OLTP, OLAP and their hybrid, plus time-series, document, graph and search access patterns — and the language must have expressive power at least equal to SQL's, with the banking constructs a domain library over a fully general relational core.

Five questions make this scientific rather than merely constructive.

**Q1 — Correctness under absence.** When a view has evicted part of its state, what must be true of the base, the epochs and the reconstruction procedure for the evicted-then-reconstructed answer to be *indistinguishable* from the never-evicted answer, and for invariants like conservation of money to survive the round trip — including under the five anomalies Noria's partial state must exclude?

**Q2 — The price of consistency under absence.** Each rung of a consistency ladder from bounded staleness to strict serializability imposes costs on a partially stateful runtime. Where exactly does partial materialization stop being cheaper than full materialization, and is that frontier a property of the history, the workload, or the consistency level demanded?

**Q3 — The policy question.** Given the frontier, what algorithm chooses materialization mode per view and key range, and what can be proved about it? Online-algorithm theory supplies both the shape of the question (rent-or-buy: 2-competitive deterministic, e/(e−1) randomized [Karlin et al.]) and the reason the classical answers do not transfer unmodified (upqueries have latency, so hits are not homogeneous in cost — the *delayed hits* problem, where even Belady's offline-optimal policy is not latency-optimal [Atre et al., SIGCOMM '20]).

**Q4 — Static checkability.** Which invariants — conservation, currency discipline, idempotency, bitemporal well-formedness, per-view consistency contracts, retention, confidentiality — can be moved into a type-and-effect system so that violations are compile-time errors rather than runtime incidents; and what exactly does the resulting soundness theorem guarantee?

**Q5 — Generality.** What does it mean, rigorously, to claim a new language is a replacement for SQL, and can that claim be proved rather than asserted?

Q5 deserves an immediate answer, because the loose form of the claim is the first thing a reviewer will attack. SQL has no single formal semantics against which "SQL-complete" can be defined. This thesis therefore defines its generality claim as the conjunction of three precise statements, developed in Section 4.7 and Appendix H: (i) *relational completeness* in Codd's sense, which is table stakes [Codd, RJ987, 1972]; (ii) *fixpoint completeness* — Niles expresses exactly the PTIME queries over the base, using the Immerman–Vardi theorem, whose ordering hypothesis the epoch-ordered ledger supplies for free [Immerman 1982/1986; Vardi 1982]; and (iii) a *translation theorem*: a total, semantics-preserving compilation of a stated SQL fragment into the Niles IR, proved by structural induction and tested by golden-file equivalence. Where the thesis wants the strongest possible statement of "general-purpose language, not a sublanguage," the reference point is Chandra–Harel computable queries [Chandra & Harel, 1980]. "Replaces SQL" is a positioning claim; these three are the provable ones.

## 1.4 New Approach

The approach decomposes the system around one new abstraction and builds both a theory and an instrument for it.

The **reconstructible epoch-anchored view (REV)** is a versioned, partially materialized derived state defined over an immutable, epoch-ordered base. The base commits in epochs; each epoch's hash chains to its predecessor, and the epoch sequence defines the only timeline the system knows. A REV is the triple of (i) a query — a DBSP-compatible circuit over the base; (ii) a *partial* materialization in which each key sits at a point of an **absence lattice** (never-computed, hole, pending, present-with-anchor); and (iii) a *serve contract* declaring consistency, freshness, materialization mode, memory budget, retention and confidentiality. A read at level ℓ either finds a sufficiently fresh present entry or triggers an upquery that reconstructs the key from the base prefix at the *requested epoch*. Because the base is immutable and totally ordered, reconstruction is a *pure function of a base prefix and an epoch*, which is what makes equivalence with the never-evicted execution provable rather than hoped — and what makes Noria's five anomalies inexpressible rather than defended against.

The general case is deliberately domain-free: an authoritative base relation with committed, append-only writes, and derived views over it. The ledger is that general case instantiated — a base whose rows are postings, whose commit rule enforces per-currency zero-sum, whose keys carry idempotency, and whose types carry money and two time axes. Every theorem is stated over the general core and then instantiated for money. The separation would be tested by building a non-financial conservation domain with no kernel changes; **that experiment is designed and has not been run**, and §6.6 records what stands in its way — seven banking forms are keywords in the compiler's registry rather than a library over it. H-S8's status line carries the same statement.

On this abstraction the thesis erects, in order: a versioned partial-state algebra and its reconstruction theorem (C1); the Eviction–Consistency Frontier Theorem bounding when partiality pays (C2); a complexity theory pricing each rung of the ladder in workload parameters (C3); a consistency-effect calculus whose soundness theorem transfers the algebra's guarantees to programs (C4); an adaptive materialization calculus — **specified and not built** — choosing per view and key range among absent, demand, full, spilled and tiered state (C5); and a generality result giving the expressiveness claim a provable form (C6).

Four foundational hypotheses justify the shape of the design before any code: streaming data is unbounded (H-F1); a table is the integral of its changelog and a changelog the derivative of a table, a duality this thesis proves within DBSP's operator algebra (H-F2); languages and DBMSs should align with these facts rather than simulate finiteness on top of them (H-F3); and the aligned shape is an immutable, fully retained base plus dynamic derived state, proved sound by a reconstruction-equivalence theorem (H-F4).

## 1.5 Objectives

### 1.5.1 General Objective

To construct and validate a formal theory of versioned, partially materialized derived state over an immutable, epoch-ordered base — the reconstructible epoch-anchored view — characterizing when demand-driven materialization is correct, what it costs at each consistency level, where it provably stops paying, and how a runtime should choose; and to embody the theory in a general-purpose language (Niles) and streaming DBMS (Nilestream) precise enough to test every claim on a purpose-built core-banking benchmark and a conservation-of-money suite.

### 1.5.2 Specific Objectives

1. Formalize the general core — authoritative base relations, committed writes, epochs, visibility frontiers — and the REV over it, with a versioned partial-state algebra compatible with DBSP's Z-set semantics.
2. Formalize the meaning of a key's absence (the absence lattice) and of the double-entry invariant, and give an *executable reference semantics* that serves as the correctness oracle.
3. State and prove the epoch-anchored reconstruction theorem and its conservation corollary: eviction followed by reconstruction can neither create nor destroy money.
4. Define the epoch-based consistency ladder as formal predicates over base prefixes and view versions, in the predicate-decomposition style of the consistency-model literature, and design the consistency-and-invariant protocol that implements it.
5. State and prove the Eviction–Consistency Frontier Theorem, locating where consistent reads under eviction cease to be cheaper than full materialization.
6. Develop the complexity theory of consistency under partial materialization: per-rung cost bounds parameterized by workload shape, with matching lower bounds in a stated model, proving the price is workload-shaped rather than history-shaped.
7. Design the consistency-effect calculus and prove Niles soundness: well-typed programs conserve value per currency, cannot mismatch currencies, and cannot overdraw without an authorization capability.
8. Build the adaptive materialization optimizer over the mode lattice {absent, demand, full, spilled, tiered}, with competitive analysis against the online-algorithms literature and an explicit account of why classical results need modification under upquery latency.
9. Design and implement Niles: a general-purpose, SQL-first, Rust-fallback language with novel constructs for base/ledger declaration, money, bitemporality and value dates, idempotency keys, materialization boundaries, per-view consistency, immutability, retention and confidentiality; with the banking layer as a library.
10. Establish the generality result: relational completeness, fixpoint completeness over the epoch-ordered base, and a proved SQL-fragment translation; and demonstrate coverage of OLTP, OLAP/HTAP, time-series, document, graph and search access patterns on the general relational core.
11. Implement Nilestream: ledger write path; partial-state read-model runtime over a typed IR that is the stable contract between language and engine; full-traceable lineage; MySQL/PostgreSQL wire compatibility for incremental adoption.
12. Build the core-banking benchmark and the runnable conservation-of-money reference model; specify the experiments that would validate or refute every hypothesis, and draw the phase diagram of demand materialization from measurement.
13. Delimit the approach honestly: threats to validity, the workloads where it loses, and the assumptions on which each proof depends.

## 1.6 Hypotheses

Every hypothesis is stated with its independent variable (IV), dependent variable (DV), control variables (CV), and its method — experimental, argumentative or formal. Foundational hypotheses ground the design; system hypotheses are the falsifiable claims about the instrument. **Measurements have been taken, and some of them refuted the claim they were testing**: Section 1.9 states the status of every claim one by one, Chapter 9 reports what was measured, and where a claim is untested Chapter 9 gives its protocol and pre-registered prediction instead. The sentence this replaces read "no measurements have been taken yet" and stood while §9.1 opened with "measurements that were actually taken".

### 1.6.1 Foundational Hypotheses

**H-F1 — Streaming data is unbounded, and finite-assuming designs pay for it.** The inputs of record-keeping systems are unbounded, append-only, time-ordered event sequences; any bound is an artifact of an observation window. A design whose correctness or cost model assumes a finite relation incurs a penalty that grows with the accumulated input.

*IV:* the design paradigm (finite-relation-first vs. stream-first), held constant within each run; and the accumulated input volume / arrival duration of a live-generating source.
*DV:* per-answer compute cost and resident memory as a function of accumulated input.
*CV:* the query set, the hardware, and the account-access skew, all held fixed.
*Method:* argumentative, with a long-horizon experiment. The argument is a mismatch catalog (Section 3.16): each way a finite-first design must simulate unbounded reality — CDC to re-derive a changelog the engine just destroyed by in-place update; triggers to approximate what retention gives exactly; application-level version fencing where anchors are absent. The experiment runs both paradigms against a live-generating source and regresses per-answer cost and resident memory on accumulated input, predicting a non-zero slope for the finite-first stack and a slope statistically indistinguishable from zero for the stream-first one.
*Falsifier:* a natural record-keeping workload whose event count is intrinsically bounded independent of window; or a finite-first design exhibiting a zero slope on the same measurement.
*Status:* **not measured.** §1.9.1's table names the instrument that does not exist.

**H-F2 — Stream–relation duality: a relation is the integral of its changelog, and a changelog the derivative of a relation.** The primitive input is an unbounded, append-only, time-ordered stream of change events; a relation is a derived, point-in-time projection of that stream.

*IV:* the derivation path taken to obtain a state — direct materialization of the relation, versus integration of its changelog; and the inverse direction, differentiation of a relation followed by replay.
*DV:* value equivalence of the two paths, compared as canonical Z-sets at every epoch.
*CV:* the underlying relation corpus, the epoch sequence, and the operator semantics, held identical across paths.
*Method:* formal, with an executable mirror. The proof is stated inside DBSP's algebra, where integration and differentiation are literal inverses: DBSP's Theorem 2.20 states that "Integration and differentiation are inverses of each other: ∀s. I(D(s)) = D(I(s)) = s" [Budiu et al., PVLDB '23], with I(s)[t] = Σ_{i≤t} s[i] and D(s)[t] = s[t] − s[t−1]. The thesis's contribution here is not the operator identity but its use: Section 3.16 proves the *asymmetry* — that integration to a final state is non-injective on histories while a retained changelog determines every intermediate state, the order and the audit trail — which is what makes the stream, not the relation, the correct primitive. CQL supplies the older architectural statement of the same correspondence, defining relations as time-varying mappings and giving stream-to-relation, relation-to-relation and relation-to-stream operator classes [Arasu et al., VLDB J. 2006]; note that CQL states a *reduction*, not a duality theorem, so the theorem is attributed to the DBSP algebra and the systems-level duality to Sax et al.'s treatment of streams and tables as "two sides of the same coin" [Sax et al., BIRTE '18]. The executable mirror is a property test asserting Z-set equality at every epoch across both derivation paths.
*Falsifier:* an epoch at which the two paths disagree as canonical Z-sets, for a query in the covered class.
*Status:* **proved**, with an executable mirror. §1.9.1.

**H-F3 — Alignment: a database language and DBMS should be built for H-F1 and H-F2.** A stream-first system can still be SQL-complete in the precise senses of Section 4.7, whereas a finite-first system cannot avoid bolting the stream back on.

*IV:* architectural alignment (stream-first single engine vs. finite-first stack plus CDC, bus, cache and warehouse).
*DV:* the operational surface — number of independently failing components, lines of hand-written consistency code, and the count of anomaly classes admitted from a fixed taxonomy — alongside latency and throughput.
*CV:* the workload set, the functional requirements to be met, and SQL-completeness held constant across both stacks.
*Method:* argumentative with a comparative audit whose counting rules are fixed before measurement (Section 9.7). The generality half is discharged by proof (Section 4.7): the stream-first design retains full relational expressive power, so alignment costs nothing in what can be asked.
*Falsifier:* a finite-first design meeting the same requirement catalog with no additional mechanism; or a demonstration that the stream-first surface forfeits expressive power.
*Status:* **not measured** on its operational half; the generality half is proved (§4.7). §1.9.1.

**H-F4 — The aligned shape is an immutable, fully retained base plus dynamic derived state.** Given H-F1–H-F3, the design that preserves all information and still serves reads economically is a never-partial retained base plus partially materialized derived views.

*IV:* the retention status of derived state (wiped vs. un-wiped) and of the base (fully retained vs. truncated).
*DV:* value-for-value equivalence of reconstructed versus pre-wiped derived state; conservation of money across the wipe; and reconstruction cost.
*CV:* the retained base, the view definitions, and the target epoch, held fixed across the wiped and un-wiped conditions.
*Method:* formal plus experimental. The reconstruction-equivalence theorem (Theorem 4.1) proves the equivalence with a fully retained base, and Section 3.16 shows each premise fails under base truncation, making full retention necessary rather than merely sufficient. The experiment wipes derived state at a chosen epoch, reconstructs, and compares value-for-value against the un-wiped condition while measuring reconstruction cost. The architectural precedents — event sourcing and CQRS, the Lambda/Kappa debate, Datomic's immutable model, and Lucene's write-once segments — are engaged in Chapter 10 as evidence that the shape is practical, not as evidence that it is correct; correctness is the theorem's job.
*Falsifier:* any (view, epoch, schedule) triple where reconstructed and pre-wiped states differ, or where conservation fails across the wipe.
*Status:* **measured** (E1, E4) atop Theorem 4.1. §1.9.1.

### 1.6.2 System Hypotheses

**H-S1 — Partiality pays when memory is dear, at an interior budget.** *(Restated after measurement; the previous formulation — "partiality pays on skew, and the advantage grows with skew" — is retained and refuted in Appendix J.9.)* Partially stateful read models hold materially less resident state than full materialization and apply materially fewer maintenance deltas, at the price of reconstruction work. Whether the trade is favourable is governed by the price of memory relative to reconstruction, and the cost-minimizing memory budget is strictly interior — neither the smallest nor the largest.
*IV:* materialization policy (full, demand-LRU, demand-with-optimizer), memory budget as a fraction of the key space, and skew (**Zipf rank exponent *s***, higher = more skew). *DV:* resident-entry-epochs; deltas applied; base rows read; hit rate; read latency; upquery rate. *CV:* write rate, epoch period τ, key domain size, view definitions, hardware.
*Method:* experimental, sweeping *s* across the empirically attested range — including the flat end (*s* ≈ 0.55–0.7, reported for two of Meta's production workloads) and not only the skewed end (*s* ≈ 1–2.5, reported for Twitter's in-memory caches) — and sweeping the **memory price** rather than fixing it, since any single fixed weighting smuggles the conclusion into the premise.
*Status:* **measured (§9.3).** The directional half of the original claim is refuted: as skew rises, full materialization's own footprint shrinks faster than partial's, so the memory advantage moves *against* partiality (12:1 at *s* = 0.5 falling to 2.7:1 at *s* = 1.3) even as partiality's reconstruction penalty falls. Skew sets the shape of the trade, not its direction.
*A notation warning that is part of the hypothesis.* The symbol α denotes at least three different quantities in this literature — a power-law density exponent, the Pareto shape parameter, and loosely the Zipf exponent — and for the Pareto shape parameter, higher means a *thinner* tail and *less* skew. Since the headline empirical object of this thesis is a phase diagram parameterized by skew, the axis is stated as the Zipf rank exponent *s*, with its direction given explicitly, throughout.
**H-S2 — The frontier is real and sits where the theorem says.** As the demanded consistency level rises toward strict serializability, and/or skew flattens, and/or upquery latency grows relative to the request rate, the measured cost of partial materialization crosses that of full materialization within the band predicted by the Eviction–Consistency Frontier Theorem.
*IV:* consistency level ℓ; skew α; memory budget; and the delayed-hit ratio Z (upquery service time divided by mean inter-arrival time), the parameter the caching-with-delayed-hits literature identifies as decisive. *DV:* total serving cost of partial vs. full materialization and the empirical crossover locus. *CV:* view family, write rate, epoch period.
*Method:* experimental-confirmatory against a theorem that predicts a region, not a point; agreement within the stated band confirms, disagreement refutes and is reported.
*Status:* **partly measured.** A crossover exists and was located; the theorem's *band* was never derived, because Θ(Z) has no stated constant. `results/E16-band.md` pre-registers the one-sided prediction that is derivable. §1.9.1.

**H-S3 — The price of consistency is workload-shaped, not history-shaped — *given a bounding mechanism*.** *(Restated after measurement; see Appendix J.10.)* Holding workload shape fixed, the marginal cost of each consistency rung is invariant to total base length, **provided reconstruction folds from a per-key checkpoint rather than from genesis.** Without that mechanism the claim is false: reconstruction cost grows with history even when the key space grows in proportion, because under skewed access a hot key retains a roughly constant share of a growing traffic total.
*IV:* base length (event count); checkpoint interval *C*; and, complementarily, view fan-out and hot-key churn with base length pinned. *DV:* base rows read per reconstruction; marginal cost per rung. *CV:* workload shape, memory budget, epoch period, hardware.
*Method:* experimental, sweeping base length over two orders of magnitude with workload shape fixed, in three conditions — no checkpointing, key space grown in proportion, and checkpointing at several intervals.
*Status:* **measured (§9.4.1), and the mechanism it forced is promoted to a named contribution (SC7).** Without checkpoints, cost grew 64× as history grew 64×; per-key anchor indices reduced the constant by 80–112× and changed nothing asymptotically; at C = 16, cost was flat at ≈ 8.5 base rows across a 64× increase in history, against a predicted C/2 + 1 = 9. A separate measurement (§9.4.3) locates the rung tax precisely: it falls on *maintenance frequency*, scaling as roughly 1/k in the staleness allowance, and is invisible on the read path.
**H-S4 — Niles soundness holds in the implementation, not only in the calculus.** No execution of a well-typed Niles program on Nilestream — including under crash-recovery, eviction, reconstruction, concurrency and adversarial schedules — violates conservation, currency separation, or authorized-overdraft.
*IV:* fault schedule and program corpus. *DV:* count of violations detected by the independent executable reference oracle. *CV:* seed-controlled workloads, engine configuration.
*Method:* experimental falsification atop a formal proof; any violation is diagnosed as either an implementation defect or a model gap, and reported either way.
*Status:* **partly measured.** The checker is falsified against a mutant corpus; no *execution* campaign exists. §1.9.1.

**H-S5 — Generality does not forfeit the floor.** The ledger write path sustains competitive strictly serializable posting throughput with hash chaining and synchronous durability, and the wire-compatibility surfaces serve unmodified MySQL/PostgreSQL clients for the benchmark's query set.
*IV:* client surface and durability mode. *DV:* sustained posting throughput; commit latency; compatibility pass rate. *CV:* hardware, batch size, epoch period.
*Method:* experimental, against published baselines and the Phase-0 floor. Vendor performance figures — including TigerBeetle's marketing claims — are explicitly *not* used as baselines; only reproducible measurements on stated hardware count.
*Status:* **partly measured.** The throughput half is measured and two contract rows are NOT MET (§9.14.1); the client-compatibility half has no pass rate and no MySQL listener. §1.9.1.

**H-S6 — Static checkability subsumes runtime policing.** For the invariant classes the effect calculus covers, moving checks to compile time removes the corresponding class of runtime failures at no measurable runtime cost, whereas imposing the same invariants on SQL baselines via triggers and constraints has measurable cost.
*IV:* enforcement site (Niles types vs. runtime constraints vs. none). *DV:* runtime overhead; residual violation count; compile time. *CV:* program corpus, workload. The three are measured by E14 (§9.14.3), the mutant corpus (§9.2), and **E26** (`results/E26-compile-cost.md`) respectively; E26 also establishes the premise the runtime-overhead half rests on, that the front end runs once per distinct statement and not per execution.
*Method:* comparative experiment on ported programs, plus the argument that a compile-time rejection is a categorically stronger artifact than a runtime abort.
*Status:* **not measured.** Neither side of the comparison has been run. §1.9.1.

**H-S7 — The optimizer beats fixed policies and approaches its competitive bound.** The adaptive materialization optimizer, choosing among absent/demand/full/spilled/tiered per view and key range, achieves lower total cost than any single fixed policy across a workload suite, and its measured competitive ratio against an offline optimum stays within the analytical bound of Section 4.6.
*IV:* policy (each fixed mode; LRU-only; the optimizer) and workload phase structure. *DV:* total cost (CPU + memory + upquery traffic + SLO violations); measured ratio to the computed offline optimum. *CV:* contracts, budgets, hardware.
*Method:* experimental, including workloads with phase changes, where adaptivity should matter most; the offline optimum is computed by dynamic programming on the recorded trace.
*Status:* **not measured.** The optimizer is specified and not built, and there is no offline optimum to measure against. §1.9.1.

**H-S8 — Generality across workload classes.** One engine and one language serve OLTP, analytical rollups, time-series, document, graph and search access patterns on the general relational core, with the banking layer implemented as a library and no kernel changes.
*IV:* workload class. *DV:* expressibility (does it compile), correctness against the oracle, and whether any kernel change was required (audited in the change log). *CV:* the engine build, the language version.
*Method:* constructive demonstration plus differential testing; the audit of kernel changes is the falsifier.
*Status:* **not measured.** The non-financial domain that would falsify it does not exist. §1.9.1.

**H-S9 — Lineage is complete and affordable.** ***Withdrawn.*** The hypothesis was that every published answer can be traced to the base facts that produced it and re-derived bit-for-bit, at a bounded overhead, with lineage mode (off, key-level, full how-provenance) as the independent variable.

It is withdrawn rather than left unmeasured, because there is no instrument and no prospect of one within this project: `nilestream-lineage` was three `pub mod` lines over three one-line files, and has been deleted from the workspace (`docs/ROADMAP.md`). With no lineage mode to switch between there is no independent variable, so there is nothing to hold constant and nothing to vary — a hypothesis in that state is not "not measured", it is not a hypothesis. What survives is Remark 4.1.2, which says what the algebra would give over the RA⁺ fragment and is careful to claim nothing about the rest, and §12, which lists lineage as future work with the semiring-factorization route intact.

**H-S10 — Holds and hot accounts do not break the model.** Available-balance semantics (posted minus holds) and highly contended accounts are served without violating conservation or the declared consistency contracts.
*IV:* contention level on hot accounts; hold volume; consistency rung of the authorization view. *DV:* authorization latency and throughput; invariant violations; APSN-class discrepancies between available and ledger balance views. *CV:* account population, skew, epoch period.
*Method:* experimental, against the coordination-avoidance theory that bounds what is possible: conservation is a sum-equality invariant and is I-confluent, whereas "balance ≥ 0" is provably not [Bailis et al., PVLDB '15], so the authorization path must coordinate and the design must make that cost explicit rather than hide it.
*Status:* **not measured.** Every instrument in this repository is serial where the experiment needs concurrency. §1.9.1.

## 1.7 Contributions

### 1.7.1 Scientific Contributions

**C1 — The REV and its versioned partial-state algebra** (§4.1–4.2): a DBSP-compatible algebra of epoch-versioned, partially materialized Z-set state, with an absence lattice giving formal meaning to a key's absence, and the epoch-anchored reconstruction theorem; corollary: eviction and reconstruction can neither create nor destroy money. The proof discharges, by construction rather than by protocol, the five anomalies partial-state dataflow must otherwise exclude.

**C2 — The Eviction–Consistency Frontier Theorem** (§4.3): a CAP/PACELC-style impossibility result bounding where strict-serializable partial materialization is provably no cheaper than full materialization, parameterized by skew, memory budget, write rate and upquery latency.

**C3 — A complexity theory of consistency under partial materialization** (§4.4): upper and lower bounds pricing each rung of the consistency ladder in workload parameters, proving the price of each rung is workload-shaped rather than history-shaped.

**C4 — The consistency-effect calculus and the Niles soundness theorem** (§4.5): a type-and-effect system in which conservation, currency discipline, idempotency and authorization are theorems about all well-typed programs, with an explicit account of which guarantees are inherited from established results and which are new.

**C5 — The adaptive materialization calculus and optimizer** (§4.6): a formal mode lattice {absent, demand, full, spilled, tiered}, the cost model that orders it, an online algorithm choosing per view and key range subject to consistency and freshness contracts, and its competitive analysis — including why the classical caching results must be modified for upquery latency and for heterogeneous reconstruction cost.

**SC7 — Bounded reconstruction: per-key checkpoints, and the bound C/2 + 1** (§9.4.1): reconstruction cost is bounded by a per-key checkpoint interval C rather than by history length, in expectation over anchors uniform between checkpoints — and is *not* bounded without that mechanism. It is numbered `SC7` rather than `C7` because it was not designed and then tested: it was **produced by an experiment that refuted C3's cost law**, and the prefix is a reminder that a contribution arriving that way is a different kind of object from one stated in advance. §9.4.1 measures it flat at ≈ 8.5 base rows across a 64× increase in history against a predicted 9.

**C6 — A generality result for the language** (§4.7): a rigorous formulation and proof of the expressiveness claim — relational completeness, fixpoint completeness over the epoch-ordered base via the Immerman–Vardi theorem, and a semantics-preserving translation of a stated SQL fragment — replacing the unprovable assertion "replaces SQL" with statements that can be checked.

### 1.7.2 Engineering Contributions

**E1 — Niles**, a general-purpose, state-aware data language: SQL-first syntax, Rust fallback, and novel constructs for authoritative bases and ledgers, `Money` indexed by currency with per-currency scale, bitemporal types and value dates, idempotency keys, per-view `serve` contracts (consistency, freshness, materialization mode, memory budget, retention), immutability and confidentiality annotations — specified in Appendix B with a consolidated grammar.

**E2 — Nilestream**, a from-scratch streaming DBMS: hash-chained epoch-ordered ledger write path; partial-state read-model runtime with anchored upqueries; a typed IR that is the stable contract between language and engine; full lineage; MySQL/PostgreSQL wire compatibility for incremental adoption.

**E3 — The executable reference semantics**: a small, auditable oracle defining correctness for the whole system, together with the conservation-of-money suite and fault campaigns built on it — reusable by any future system claiming ledger-grade semantics.

**E4 — The core-banking benchmark**: a reproducible workload coupling ledger-grade writes to a skewed, open-ended derived-read fleet, including available-balance-with-holds, as-of balances on both time axes, BCBS-239-style rollups, statements, and fraud/AML features.

**E5 — The phase diagram** as a design tool: a measured characterization of when demand materialization pays, directly consumable by practitioners sizing read-model fleets.

## 1.8 Thesis Statement

*Versioned, partially materialized derived state over an immutable, epoch-ordered, hash-chained base admits a formal algebra in which reconstruction is exact, invariants are preserved, the cost of every consistency level is a function of workload shape rather than history length, the choice of materialization mode is an online decision with provable guarantees, and the limits of the approach form a provable frontier — and this theory can be embodied in a general-purpose language and DBMS, with expressive power at least SQL's and banking as a domain library, precise enough to falsify it.*

## 1.9 Scope, Non-Goals, and the Status of Claims

**In scope:** the theory of the REV; the language and its type-and-effect discipline; the single-node engine design and its distributed extension; the benchmark and oracle; the honest characterization of where the approach wins and loses.

**Out of scope, declared:** Byzantine fault tolerance (the hash chain gives tamper *evidence*, not tamper *proofing* — see below); consensus protocol novelty (the design reuses established replication); adversarial multi-operator settings; and application-tier concerns beyond the language's declared scope tiers.

**On tamper evidence.** The guarantee a hash chain provides must be stated exactly, because the literature is explicit and overclaiming here would be a real vulnerability. RFC 6962 puts it plainly: "The logs do not themselves prevent misissue, but they ensure that interested parties … can detect such misissuance." QLDB's documentation scopes its verification the same way: what is proved is that a revision "wasn't altered between the time that you saved this digest and when you run the verification." The thesis therefore claims: *tamper-evidence relative to an externally retained digest, conditional on someone checking* — not immutability as a physical property.

**Status of claims.** This is a theory-and-design thesis with a research prototype. Its claims fall into three tiers, and the thesis does not blur them.

*Proved.* The theorems of Chapters 3 and 4 are paper proofs in stated models, with the verification status of each — proved, machine-checkable, assumed, or restricted — tabulated in Section 3.15. None is mechanized; that is named as future work rather than implied.

*Measured.* Sections 9.1–9.4 report results from a prototype (Appendix K) implementing the mechanisms: epoch-ordered commit under a per-currency zero-sum rule, hash chaining, anchor indices, checkpoints, partial materialization over the absence lattice, anchored upqueries and eviction policies. Every number there was produced by the run that reports it, on the platform stated in §9.1.2, with five fixed seeds and machine-independent counted-work units. **Three of those measurements contradicted claims made in earlier drafts of this thesis, and are reported as contradictions** (§9.3.4, §9.4.1, §9.4.4).

*Neither.* Everything requiring durability, concurrency, distribution or the compiler is marked *to be measured*, with its protocol and pre-registered prediction. **No number anywhere in this thesis is invented to fill such a cell.** Two comparisons against another database system are claimed and reported — E14 (§6.10.3) and E16 (§9.14.1) — and E16's durable rows are measured with `synchronous_commit = on` against `SyncPolicy::Always` on the same device, so the prototype is not standing in for a durable system: it is one, on the write path, and the rows that are unmet say so.

*On figures quoted from the literature.* Each carries its original experimental context, and vendor performance claims lacking a disclosed methodology, hardware specification and durability setting are named as such and excluded from use as baselines.
