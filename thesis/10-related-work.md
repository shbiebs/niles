# 10. Related Work

Chapter 2 gave the inheritance; this chapter gives the differences, family by family, and ends with the synthesis that locates the gap.

## 10.1 Purpose-Built Ledgers

TigerBeetle demonstrates that a fixed-schema, double-entry ledger can be extraordinarily disciplined: accounts, transfers and ledgers as the only primitives; the transfer identifier serving as an idempotency key; non-negativity expressed as a per-account flag rather than application logic; two-phase pending transfers that reserve amounts and are resolved by *appending* rather than mutating; static memory allocation; deterministic execution; and data that is "immutable, checksummed, and hash-chained." Every one of those choices is validated by this thesis's theory, and several are adopted directly. What TigerBeetle does not offer is a general query surface, a derived-view runtime, a language, or any account of read-side economics — reads are point lookups, and everything else is another system's problem.

Uber's estate splits the problem the same way but demonstrates the read side too. Its payments platform states the zero-sum rule as ensuring "no money can ever be created or destroyed," with adjustments captured as additional money orders rather than modifications. LedgerStore, its immutable storage layer, ships *three* index classes over one base — strongly consistent (two-phase commit, used for credit-card authorization holds), eventually consistent (payment history), and time-range indexes enabling tiering. That is per-view consistency in production, chosen by hand. This thesis's contribution relative to it is to give that choice a semantics, a contract language, a proof that reconstruction is exact, and an optimizer that makes the choice from a cost model rather than from architectural intuition.

Square's Books contributes the append-only schema discipline and, notably, the observation that materializing the payout balance makes it "a single row in our database [that] requires no complex grouping or aggregation" — a totally materialized REV, chosen for a hot view, which is exactly what this thesis's optimizer would select for that access pattern. Modern Treasury contributes the design-space disagreement about whether pending transactions are mutable, which Section 3.19 resolves in the stricter direction and explains why.

Immutable and bitemporal databases (Datomic, XTDB) and journal-first ledger services (QLDB) establish that retention, time travel and cryptographic verification are practical, and QLDB in particular supplies the precise scoping language this thesis adopts for its verification claims. **"Commercially viable" is withdrawn as a description of QLDB**: AWS announced its deprecation, with end of support in 2025 and no new accounts accepted [AWS, QLDB end of support], which is a fact about the category worth reporting rather than eliding. The correct reading is that the *technique* is demonstrated and the *product* was retired — and the second is evidence about the market for a standalone ledger database, which §11 discusses under project identity. **Three nearer neighbours than half of what is cited above, and none of them were engaged.**

*DynaMat* [Kotidis & Roussopoulos, SIGMOD '99] maintains a *dynamic* view pool: results of past queries are materialized, kept under a space constraint, and evicted by a cost-benefit rule, with an admission policy deciding what to keep as queries arrive. That is the same economics as C5's mode calculus, one abstraction lower — the unit is a query result rather than a key range of a view, and there is no consistency dimension at all. What the REV has that DynaMat does not is the *anchor*: a DynaMat fragment is a cached answer with no version, so it is either fresh or discarded, and the question this thesis is about — what a partially resident view may honestly say about a key it does not hold — cannot be posed.

*Semantic caching* [Dar et al., VLDB '96] is nearer still, and is the closest prior statement of "materialize what was read". A client keeps semantic regions describing what it holds; a new query is split into a probe answerable locally and a remainder sent to the server. The remainder *is* an upquery. The differences are the two this thesis turns on: the regions describe a mutable base, so a region is invalidated rather than re-anchored, and correctness is defined against the server's current state rather than against a named epoch — so there is nothing for a consistency contract to attach to.

*Hyder* [Bernstein et al., CIDR '11] is the architectural neighbour: a single shared log is the database, every server rolls the log forward into its own indexed state, and transactions are validated by a deterministic meld against the log order. "The log is the database" is Hyder's sentence before it is this thesis's, and it is credited as such. Hyder's derived state is *whole* — each server materializes the index it needs in full — and its research question is the meld algorithm's throughput rather than what may be left out. Partiality, versioned absence and a per-view contract are what this thesis adds to a structure Hyder established.

Blockchain systems share the hash-chained append-only base but solve an adversarial-consensus problem explicitly out of scope here, paying for Byzantine tolerance in throughput and expressiveness; Niles targets the trusted-operator institution, where the interesting problems are economics and semantics rather than adversarial agreement.

## 10.2 Distributed Strict-Serializable SQL and Deterministic Databases

Spanner, CockroachDB and FoundationDB set the standard of rigor for the write path; the deterministic school — Calvin and Aria — is closer kin still, since pre-agreeing a total order over transaction inputs makes execution a deterministic function of that order and removes the need for a distributed commit vote. Aria's advance over its predecessors, dropping the requirement that read/write sets be known in advance, is directly relevant to a system whose transactions include application logic.

The differences: their history is disposable and audit is external; their derived reads are either per-query recomputation or eagerly total materialized views; and their language carries none of the static invariants. The relationship is complementary rather than competitive — this thesis takes global ordering as an available answer and asks what the read side and the language should be, and its reconstruction-equivalence theorem is, in a precise sense, the read-side analogue of the replica-equivalence property that determinism gives the write side.

## 10.3 Incremental View Engines and Partial State

Materialize, Feldera, RisingWave and Flink SQL maintain views incrementally with strong internal consistency; DBToaster pioneered higher-order incrementalization; timely and differential dataflow supply the substrate, including shared arrangements for cross-query state sharing.

Materialize is the sharpest comparison and deserves precision. It already ships bounded staleness (parameterized by duration), serializable and strict serializable isolation, and its documentation is explicit that strict serializability is what removes the anomaly in which a transaction reading an expensive view fails to observe rows visible to an earlier transaction that read the base table. So the *ladder* is not this thesis's novelty. The differentiator is that all of those guarantees are over *totally* maintained state: a view's memory cost is the size of its output. Because Materialize never evicts, it never has to ask when eviction stops paying — Theorem 4.2 has no analogue there because it has no reason to exist there.

Noria is the one system with partial state and upqueries, and Section 4.8 gives the point-by-point delta. Two aspects of Noria's own framing are worth repeating here because they define the opening: its stated guarantee is that "if writes quiesce, all external views eventually hold results that are the same as if the queries had been executed directly against the base table data," and its dissertation lists stronger consistency and transactions as future work. The five anomalies that dissertation names are the obligation list this thesis's Theorem 4.1 discharges by construction rather than by protocol.

The general position: this thesis is DBSP-compatible by construction and adds the dimension none of these engines model — partiality with versioned absence, anchored reconstruction, per-view contracts over a shared authoritative timeline, and a cost theory of serving under eviction.

## 10.4 Core-Banking Engineering Patterns

Industrial core-banking platforms and the payments literature converge on the same folk architecture — immutable postings, derived balances, event-driven projections — implemented as engineering discipline without formal guarantees. The regulatory record supplies the failure evidence: the FDIC and CFPB descriptions of authorize-positive-settle-negative practices document a consumer-harm pattern that arises structurally from two derived views of one ledger disagreeing at a decision point.

This thesis can be read as the missing theory for that folk architecture. What those systems assert in runbooks — that balances derive from postings, that corrections are appends, that some views may lag — Chapter 4 proves or prices, and Chapter 6 makes declarable and checkable. The strongest evidence that the folk architecture is right is that the most disciplined production ledgers have independently converged on the same primitives; the strongest evidence that it is insufficient is that they each re-solve the read side by hand.

## 10.5 Query Languages as SQL Alternatives

Datalog and its differential variant, PRQL, Malloy, EdgeQL, Morel, Logica, LINQ, KQL and Rel improve compositionality, pipeline order, abstraction or host-language integration. A survey of their own positioning yields a finding this thesis leans on: none claims formal SQL-superset expressive power, several are bounded above by SQL because they compile to it, and one major entrant is read-only by design. Rel is closest in ambition — explicitly rejecting the sublanguage model in favour of a full language, with relational algebra demonstrated as a library — but argues from design philosophy rather than from theorem.

Niles's position is an ergonomic reform *and* a semantic extension: the only language in this family whose type system speaks the database's consistency, temporal, monetary, lineage and confidentiality vocabulary, and — via Theorem 4.6 — the only one to state its expressiveness claim as something provable rather than asserted. The standards lineage is engaged rather than ignored: SQL:2011's system-versioned tables already forbid user modification of historical rows, SQL:2023 added property-graph queries, and ISO published GQL in 2024 as a standalone language rather than another SQL part — evidence that "new language" is a legitimate answer when the model differs, and that read-only sublanguages and full languages are recognized as different artifacts.

The strongest opposing position is "One SQL to Rule Them All," which argues that streaming needs only time-varying relations, event-time semantics and a small set of materialization keywords added to standard SQL. Section 6.10 concedes its query-semantics half and locates the disagreement precisely: SQL can be extended to say what to compute over streams; it cannot be extended to refuse programs that would lose money.

## 10.6 The Log, Event Sourcing, and Stream-First Architectures

The log-centric school, event sourcing and CQRS, and the Lambda/Kappa debate are closest in spirit, and CQL is their academic ancestor, having defined relations as time-varying mappings with explicit operator classes bridging streams and relations two decades ago. The stream-table duality of the Kafka lineage states the same correspondence at the systems level.

The delta is consistent throughout: the school provides patterns, this thesis provides semantics — exactly-once maintenance as a theorem rather than a configuration flag, per-view contracts rather than consumer-side hope, an impossibility frontier rather than sizing folklore, and a language in which the projections' invariants are checked. It is worth noting how close the industrial practice already is: Flink and Kafka both achieve their exactly-once property by committing derived state and input position atomically, which is structurally identical to anchoring a read model to an epoch. The generalization this thesis offers is to make that anchor a first-class, queryable, per-answer value rather than an internal checkpoint.

## 10.7 Partial Materialization, Caching Theory, and Self-Tuning Systems

Three literatures bear on Contribution 5. Competitive analysis of paging supplies the classical bounds and their tightness; file caching extends them to heterogeneous size and retrieval cost, which is the correct shape for reconstruction of differing depth; rent-or-buy supplies the mode-selection skeleton with its 2-competitive deterministic and e/(e−1) randomized results; and the delayed-hits work supplies the correction that matters most here — when a fill takes time, hit rate is the wrong objective, and even the offline-optimal hit-rate policy is not latency-optimal.

View and index selection supply the offline framing and its hardness, along with the (1 − 1/e) greedy guarantee whose *cost-model assumption* partial materialization violates — a violation this thesis reports as a negative result rather than eliding. Adaptive indexing supplies the principle that structure should be built in response to demand, and the cautionary datum that *how* it is built can move convergence by two orders of magnitude. Self-driving database work supplies the agenda of automated physical design.

What none of them has is a consistency dimension. Cracking reorganizes physical storage and asks no question about visibility; view selection assumes the view, once chosen, is simply correct. The novelty of Contribution 5 is deciding materialization *under a declared consistency contract*, where the same key range can be cheap on demand at one rung and unaffordable at another.

## 10.8 Provenance, Lineage, and Verifiable Structures

Semiring provenance supplies the algebra, the factorization theorem, and the specialization-by-homomorphism structure that makes lineage cheap here. ORCHESTRA is the most important prior art in this chapter, because it used exactly that formalism for exactly this purpose — incremental maintenance of derived state — and reported a measured break-even against full recomputation. Section 4.3 states the three differences (residency and consistency versus update volume; the consistency multiplier; reconstruction latency) rather than claiming novelty where there is none, and Corollary 4.1.2 is explicitly an inherited result.

Streaming provenance work (operator instrumentation with eager and lazy modes) and interactive dataflow lineage supply the implementation techniques and the overhead reference point. On the verification side, certificate transparency supplies both the Merkle machinery and the exact language for what it guarantees — detection, not prevention — and Trillian's log-backed map is the structural precedent for "authoritative log plus derived state applied only from the log," which this thesis explicitly does not claim as novel. LambdaAuth is the precedent for making an auditability property a *typing* property, which is the closest analogue to Contribution 4's move.

## 10.9 HTAP, Multi-Model, and One Size Fits All

The dialectic runs from the argument that specialized engines would displace the general RDBMS, through the main-memory rebuttal and the HTAP literature it launched, to today's multi-model systems. The HTAP architectures — dual row/column formats over one table, columnar replicas fed by a novel replication role, unified storage that hides the format from users, and the deliberately decoupled "HTAP as a service" position — are all answers to the question of how one system serves two access patterns over *mutable* state.

This thesis's position is that the question changes shape over an immutable base. Where an HTAP engine must synchronize two representations of the same mutable table, Nilestream maintains two REVs over the same immutable base, which is the same idea with a stronger correctness story and an additional partiality dimension. The multi-model precedents — binary decomposed document representation, path-indexed schema-agnostic indexing, time-series compression, immutable search segments with tombstones and merging — are adopted as *resident representations chosen by the optimizer* rather than as separate engines, which is what Theorem 4.5(a) licenses.

## 10.10 Coordination Avoidance and Contended Records

Coordination-avoidance theory and the contended-record literature are treated as constraints rather than as competition, and they are what keep this thesis's claims honest. I-confluence gives the necessary and sufficient condition for coordination-free invariant preservation and the classification that puts sum-equality on the cheap side and thresholds-under-decrement on the expensive side. Escrow, the demarcation protocol, phase reconciliation and sharded counters give four independent mechanisms that all respect the same boundary: blind, self-commuting, value-preserving operations split and scale; aggregate thresholds do not.

This convergence is the strongest external support the thesis has for the shape of its architecture. Conservation — the invariant Corollary 4.1.1 protects — is exactly the cheap kind, which is why partial materialization can be aggressive without endangering money. No-overdraft is exactly the expensive kind, which is why authorization is confined to one path, at the top rung, with reservations that are themselves immutable ledger facts.

## 10.11 Verification Practice: What "Tested" and "Verified" Can Mean

Three literatures bear on how this thesis's correctness claims should be worded, and each supplies a limit as well as a method.

**Black-box safety testing.** Jepsen's own framing is the right epistemic anchor: "we can prove the presence of bugs, but not their absence." Its bank test — simulate transfers, verify that reads always sum to the same total, optionally check non-negativity — is precisely this thesis's conservation-of-money suite, arrived at independently. The result that matters most here is a negative one: in one published analysis, the bank tests "passed consistently" while a causal-reverse anomaly showed the system was serializable but not *strictly* serializable. That forbids presenting a conservation suite as evidence for the ladder's top rung (§9.2.2), and it is the reason the correctness programme adds Elle, which infers an Adya-style dependency graph from client-observed histories and is sound in the sense that any anomaly it reports is present in every interpretation of the observation. Elle's requirement — a *traceable* data model whose version history can be recovered by reading — is satisfied by construction here, which is a genuine argument for an append-only base and one this thesis had not previously made.

**Formal specification of shipped systems.** Formal methods have found defects in production storage systems that testing did not: a model checker found a data-loss bug in a commercial key-value store whose shortest counterexample ran to 35 high-level steps, and a TLA+ specification of a commercial database's five advertised consistency levels found that two of them were indistinguishable to a client and that its bounded-staleness rung was subject to no bound at all under strongly-consistent writes. The second is both a methodological template for Chapter 3's ladder-as-predicates and a warning that a ladder can be advertised with more rungs than it has. The limit, which the same literature states plainly, is that these results are about *designs*: they do not establish that an implementation refines its specification.

**Benchmarking discipline.** The named benchmarking errors — pretending microbenchmarks represent system performance, evaluating only against oneself, omitting the platform specification, reporting relative numbers only — are the checklist Chapter 9 is written against, and §9.1.1 answers them explicitly rather than implicitly. The statistical companion is equally pointed: a survey of fifty systems papers found that a third specified no evaluation methodology at all and that only four reported confidence intervals. This thesis reports medians with ranges over five fixed seeds, separates warm from cold paths rather than blending them, and states which of the three memory layers any figure refers to.

**ReadySet**, the production descendant of the partial-state work, deserves a note here rather than in §10.3, because what is publicly documented about it is instructive by omission. Its engineering material describes the mechanism this thesis builds on — a dataflow graph in which "only the affected nodes are recomputed", upqueries that recompute from base tables on a miss, and eviction under memory pressure with reload via upquery — but its public documentation contains **no formal consistency-model statement**: no staleness bound, no read-your-writes guarantee, no defined behaviour under replication lag, and a freshness claim stated as "typically within a few milliseconds" rather than as a guarantee. That is not a criticism of the product, which is a cache and says so; it is evidence that the gap this thesis addresses — partial materialization *with* a stated contract — remains open in practice as well as in the literature.

## 10.12 Synthesis and Gap

Assembled, the families cover every ingredient and no combination. Strictly serializable ordered writes exist without read-side economics. Incremental view maintenance exists without partiality, or without authority over the base. Partial state exists without consistency, versions, or invariants. Ledgers exist without generality or language, though the best of them have converged on the right primitives. Provenance-guided incremental maintenance exists without a consistency dimension. Caching and self-tuning theory exists without any notion of a correctness contract. Language rigor exists without a database semantics to be sound for. And the log-plus-derived-state architecture exists, in production, at scale, for two decades — without a proof.

The gap this thesis fills is the intersection: one semantic object, the REV, that makes the combination coherent; six results that make it trustworthy, priced, bounded, decidable, checkable and general; and one language and engine that make it usable and falsifiable. To the author's knowledge, no prior work states or proves reconstruction-exactness under eviction over an authoritative epoch-ordered base, prices consistency rungs under partial materialization, specifies a materialization-mode calculus under a consistency contract, or types conservation of value into a general-purpose query language with a proved expressiveness result.
